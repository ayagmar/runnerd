## The service

`runnerd init` writes a user unit that runs `runnerd serve`:

```ini
[Unit]
Description=runnerd: ephemeral GitHub Actions runners
Documentation=https://ayagmar.github.io/runnerd/

[Service]
Type=exec
ExecStart="/home/dana/.local/bin/runnerd" serve --config "/home/dana/.config/runnerd/runnerd.toml"
Restart=on-failure
RestartSec=10

[Install]
WantedBy=default.target
```

```sh
systemctl --user enable --now runnerd   # start now and at login
loginctl enable-linger "$USER"          # keep it running while you are logged out
journalctl --user -u runnerd -f         # its log
systemctl --user restart runnerd        # after editing runnerd.toml by hand
systemctl --user stop runnerd           # waits for running jobs, up to systemd's stop timeout
```

A config saved from the dashboard or `PUT /v1/config` applies without a restart, except `[web]`, `history.database` and `caches.build.directory`. `runnerd stop` waits for running jobs too; `runnerd stop --force`, or a second signal, kills them.

To back up the config, copy `runnerd.toml` itself: `runnerctl config show` shows [notification](../insights/#notifications) URLs and headers as `"<secret>"`, and runnerd refuses to start with those.

Runner containers live in Docker, not in the unit: a restarted runnerd adopts the ones still running, and keeps the repositories it had paused paused.

## How it schedules

- It reads the `gh` CLI's token at start, and again when GitHub rejects it.
- Every [`poll_interval`](../configuration/#host) it asks GitHub for each repository's queued jobs.
- A job is served by the repository's pool whose `labels` include every label in its `runs-on`: one `[[repositories]]` entry, or one of [several](../configuration/#split-light-and-heavy-jobs).
- For each one it registers a just-in-time runner and starts it in a free slot of that pool's profile, if the slice's `MemoryMax` still has room for the profile's `memory`, or with [measured admission](../admission/#measured-admission) for the job's predicted peak, and under [CPU admission](../admission/#cpu-admission) if the CPU budget has room for it too. Other jobs wait in GitHub's queue.
- The runner takes one job, its container exits, and runnerd removes it. A runner with no job after 5 minutes is removed, unless it is a [warm runner](#warm-runners).
- A restarted runnerd adopts the runner containers still running and deletes this host's runners that have none.
- Before a profile's first runner it pulls the profile's `image`, logging the pull's progress.

## Warm runners

A job that finds no runner waits for the next poll, a registration and a container start: 15 to 25 seconds. A warm runner is already registered when GitHub queues the job, and takes it within a second or two.

- Each pool keeps [`warm`](../configuration/#repositories) runners (1 by default) while its repository is active: a job of it was queued in the last hour, or it is an hour of the day (UTC) its jobs came in on 3 of the last 14 days. Outside those, its warm runners go after 5 minutes idle and none starts again until a job comes.
- When a job of a workflow run is seen, the run's other jobs get runners too: in each pool, as many as that workflow's runs usually had there (the median of the last 14 days, from 3 runs), less the ones already seen.
- Warm runners start last: only once every queued job that fits has its runner, and while no job waits for a slot, memory or CPU. Each reserves its profile's `memory` (and `cpus` under CPU admission, at most the CPU budget).
- A queued job of another repository that needs the room takes it: runnerd removes as many warm runners as make the job fit and starts its runner in the same poll.
- A profile due a reseed (slot 1 ran a job) removes its warm runners once its other runners are idle, so the snapshot still gets rebuilt. The shared caches' check does the same across all profiles, at most once an hour, until it ends.
- A warm runner that ends without a job after waiting, as when Docker restarts, does not hold its repository's next runners back; a second in a row does.

`runnerctl watch`, the TUI and the dashboard show them as warm runners waiting for a job; `runnerctl status` shows them `idle`. Sizing tells the jobs a warm runner took from the others, with each kind's median wait.

## Warm caches

A slot is one concurrent job of a profile. Each job gets a new container, but the slot keeps a volume over the profile's read-only snapshot. A queued job starts in the free slot that ran the same job last (same repository, workflow and job name), else in one that ran a job of its repository, else in the lowest free slot, so a repeated job finds its own caches. It never waits for that slot, and a warm runner takes whichever job GitHub gives it. A reseed that replaces the snapshot drops every slot's own changes, and with them the preference. What a job deletes of the image, or replaces, stays deleted for every slot once it is in the snapshot. The images point every common toolchain's cache into `~/.cache`: rustup and cargo, Go modules and builds, Gradle, Maven, npm, Yarn, Bun, pip, uv, Poetry, pub, Deno and coursier.

<Caches />

| Cache | Kept |
|---|---|
| the slot's Docker images, `~/.cache`, tool cache, Android SDK | per slot, between jobs, shared by the jobs of every repository and branch on the profile: a job can tag an image, such as `postgres:16`, that a later job in the slot then runs without pulling |
| the slot's Docker build cache, BuildKit's cache mounts included | per slot, between jobs. After each job the build cache is trimmed, least recently used first, to about half the slot's share, and a store still over the share is emptied, images too. The slots together get a quarter of the free disk plus what their volumes hold, at least 1 GB and at most 20 GB per slot. A store the slot's Docker Engine does not start on, as after a crash, is emptied when the next container starts. A slot closed by a lower `slots` loses its volume at the next check of the caches, and its layers start over from the snapshot if a reseed ran while it was closed. The containers, volumes and networks a job starts go after it |
| cargo's credentials and config, Gradle's `gradle.properties` and init scripts, the Maven wrapper's settings, Hugging Face's tokens in `~/.cache` | never: no later job or record shell sees them, even from an older snapshot. Anything else a job leaves in the caches, later jobs of other repositories see ([trust boundary](../configuration/#caches-are-shared-between-repositories)) |
| the profile's snapshot | replaced by slot 1's changes once they reach [`reseed_at`](../configuration/#caches) and the profile is idle; a snapshot that would hold more than `snapshot_max` starts over from the image instead, with every slot's changes; once measured above `snapshot_max`, it starts over at the next moment the profile is idle, however little slot 1 changed. After a reseed that did not finish, as when the host went down during one, the profile's jobs run on the image alone until the next reseed starts it over |
| pnpm store, Maven repository | shared by all slots, emptied above their [`max_size`](../configuration/#caches) when no runner runs: warm runners stop for it at most hourly, at the first moment no job runs |
| Docker Hub mirror | runs while any runner does, warm ones too; stops, and its storage is emptied above `mirror.max_size`, with the shared caches' check |
| [build cache](#build-cache) | per repository, shared by every slot; written only by [the jobs that may](#build-cache): pushes, schedules and manual runs of the default branch or `build.branches`, and opted-in pull requests; its least recently used entries go above [`build.max_size`](../configuration/#caches) with the shared caches' check |

## Build cache

A Gradle task built in slot 2 is a cache miss in slot 3: each slot has its own `~/.cache`. The build cache is one store every runner reaches, kept by the daemon in `~/.cache/runnerd/build-cache`, one per repository. A pull request's build gets what `main` built, whichever slot built it.

| Tool | What it gets | When |
|---|---|---|
| Gradle | its [HTTP build cache](https://docs.gradle.org/current/userguide/build_cache.html) as the remote cache, through an init script in `$GRADLE_USER_HOME/init.d` | the build turns the build cache on (`org.gradle.caching=true` or `--build-cache`) and configures no remote cache of its own |
| sccache | its WebDAV backend (`SCCACHE_WEBDAV_ENDPOINT`) | the workflow runs it (`RUSTC_WRAPPER=sccache`) with no other backend: S3, GCS, GitHub Actions' cache and the rest take precedence |
| Bazel | `--remote_cache` in `/etc/bazel.bazelrc`, with `--remote_download_outputs=all` | the workspace's `.bazelrc` sets no `--remote_cache` of its own |

Who writes:

- A job writes only when GitHub says it runs a push, a schedule or a manual run (`workflow_dispatch`) of the repository's default branch, as GitHub said it within the last hour, or of one of [`build.branches`](../configuration/#caches), which add to it. runnerd asks GitHub which runner holds the job, what started its run, and whether the branch has the run's commit, so a tag named `main` does not pass for the branch; what the job writes in its runner's log or environment counts for nothing.
- A repository with `build_cache_writers = "pull-requests"` ([Configuration](../configuration/#repositories)) also lets its own branches' pull requests write, on the `pull_request` event only. GitHub must say a person, not a bot, opened the pull request, opened, reopened or pushed to it for this run, and started or re-ran the run, and the branch must still have the run's commit. Who made the commits is not checked: a person's pull request from a branch a bot pushed writes. A fork's pull request, Renovate's or Dependabot's, and a run a bot pushed to a person's pull request read only. Whoever can push a branch to the repository can then change what `main` builds load, so turn it on only where that is you. With strict up-to-date pull requests it pays: the merge builds the tree the pull request's last run built, so `main` finds every task in the cache. It helps only where a job's build goes through the cache, with Gradle, sccache or Bazel; Maven, Turborepo and Flutter builds gain nothing from it.
- Every other job, pull requests from forks or not, reads. Its uploads are answered as stored and dropped, so Gradle keeps the cache on for the rest of the build.
- What a job uploads before GitHub has answered, usually its first seconds, waits and is kept or dropped once it has. A job that ends first and uploaded something is asked about right after its runner is removed; its uploads are dropped only if GitHub does not answer then.
- A repository reads only its own entries.

sccache uses the shared cache instead of its disk cache in the slot, not as well as: a pull request's own compilations are not kept, so a second push to it compiles again what the first did. A Rust repository with many pull requests may do better with its own backend, or `build_cache = false`.

Each runner reaches the cache through a socket of its own, which no other runner sees; the image forwards it to `127.0.0.1:41771`. A cache that does not answer, as while runnerd restarts, fails no build: Gradle warns and builds without it. Containers a job starts itself, such as a `container:` job's or `docker build`'s, do not reach it.

The job page and `runnerctl job` show each job's hits and what it read and stored; the Caches page and `runnerctl caches`, the cache's size and its hit rate over 7 days. The [Cold cache](../insights/#insights) insight names a job whose writing runs find almost nothing in it.

To keep a repository out, set `build_cache = false` under it. A workflow turns off Gradle's part with `RUNNERD_BUILD_CACHE_URL: ""` in its `env`, and any tool's by configuring a remote cache of its own. `runnerctl caches --clean runnerd-build-cache` empties it while no runner runs a job.

### GitHub Actions' cache

With `build.actions_cache = true` under [`[caches]`](../configuration/#caches), the build cache also serves `actions/cache`, the `cache:` input of `setup-node`, `setup-java`, `setup-python` and `setup-gradle`, and Docker's `cache-from: type=gha` and `cache-to: type=gha`. No workflow changes: the image points each node action at the cache in place of GitHub's, and GitHub's cache is neither read nor written while it is on. Restores and saves stay on the host's disk, within `build.max_size` with the build cache's entries.

Entries are scoped to branches and pull requests, as on GitHub, and only [the jobs that may write](#build-cache) save:

- A push, schedule or manual run of the default branch or of `build.branches` saves into its branch, and restores from its branch, then from the default branch.
- An opted-in pull request saves into the pull request's own scope, never its head branch's, and restores from that, then from its base branch, then from the default branch.
- Every other job restores from the default branch only. Its saves say they saved, so Docker builds that save to the cache do not fail, and are dropped.
- A pull request never writes what a default-branch job restores.
- Restores at the start of a job, before GitHub has said what the job is, read the default branch. The first job of a repository after runnerd starts finds nothing then.

A save of a key its scope already has is skipped, as on GitHub. An entry may take at most a quarter of `build.max_size`, however it is uploaded. Artifacts (`upload-artifact`, `download-artifact`) still go to GitHub: runnerd passes those requests on unchanged.

It does not reach a workflow that sets `NODE_OPTIONS` itself, `container:` jobs and `docker://` actions: those use GitHub's cache. Turning it on starts the cache cold; the first default-branch run of each key saves it. Turning it off takes effect for jobs that start after the change; a job already running when runnerd restarts restores nothing and saves nothing for the rest of it, and its artifacts still upload.

## Records of failed jobs

With `record_failures` on a profile ([Configuration](../configuration/#records-of-failed-jobs)), a failed job's stopped runner is committed to an image, `runnerd-record:<job>`, before it is removed. `runnerctl shell <job>` starts a container from it and opens bash in the job's workspace, after printing the step that failed and what it ran:

```console wrap=false
$ runnerctl shell 120055991489
runnerd: the record of job 120055991489 of halyard/ledger, CI / build
It failed at step 5: Unit tests (exit code 1)
Its log shows it ran:
    ./gradlew test --no-daemon
    shell: /usr/bin/bash -e {0}
You are in /home/runner/_work/ledger/ledger, as the job left it. Not kept: the job's Docker containers and images; what it changed in ~/.cache, the tool cache and the Android SDK, which show the profile's snapshot; and what it added or changed among your home's dot-files and in /tmp (logins, and tools in ~/.local or ~/.nvm), except ~/.rustup and ~/.cargo's bin, registry and git. Exit to remove this container.
runner@4be1c0d2a7e9:~/_work/ledger/ledger$
```

`--step 3` shows step 3 instead.

- The terminal goes over the API socket and keeps the size it was opened with.
- The shell's container counts against the budget with the profile's memory cap, and is refused when the budget cannot hold it, or under [measured admission](../admission/#measured-admission) while the slice is at 90% of `MemoryMax`.
- It mounts the profile's snapshot and the shared pnpm and Maven caches read-only. While it is open, the profile does not reseed (`runnerctl reseed` answers 409) and the shared caches are not trimmed or cleaned. It is refused while the profile reseeds or the shared caches are trimmed.
- It goes when the shell exits, when the client goes away, or after 8 hours.

| What | In a record |
|---|---|
| the workspace, `_work/<repository>`, with build output and test reports | yes |
| the runner's `_diag` logs, secrets masked as the runner masks them on GitHub | yes |
| a Rust toolchain in `~/.rustup` and `~/.cargo`'s `bin`, `registry` and `git`, for a job that sets `RUSTUP_HOME` and `CARGO_HOME` there (the images put both in `~/.cache`) | yes |
| anything else the job wrote outside volumes and the places below, such as a tool unpacked in `/opt` | yes |
| other dot-files and dot-directories the job added or changed in the runner's home: logins (`.gitconfig`, `.aws`, `.gnupg`, `.docker`, `~/.cargo/credentials.toml` and `config.toml`, the runner's `.credentials`) and tools installed there (`~/.local`, `~/.nvm`, `~/.dotnet`, `~/.bun`, `~/.sdkman`); what it added or changed in `/tmp` and `/var/tmp` | never |
| the JIT config, `_work/_temp` (step scripts), the token `actions/checkout` puts in `.git/config` | never |
| the slot's caches (rustup and cargo included), tool cache, Android SDK, and the job's Docker images | no; volumes, which a shell sees as the profile's snapshot |

The runner image deletes the "never" rows when the runner exits, and runnerd refuses to keep a container whose changes still hold any of them. A step that saves a secret into the workspace or elsewhere outside those places leaves it in the record: runnerd does not read file contents.

Records are readable by anyone who can use Docker on this host (`docker run runnerd-record:<job>`), and by the user runnerd runs as, through its socket, which is the only client the socket accepts. Turn `record_failures` on only for profiles whose jobs you would let those users see.

Records take disk: committing one takes a second or two, and it takes about twice what the job wrote outside volumes, since Docker keeps the layer packed and unpacked. `runnerctl records` lists them with their limits; `runnerctl records rm <job>`, `--all` or `--older-than 2d` removes them. The job history keeps, after a record is gone, when it was made, its size, and why it went (`count`, `size`, `age` or `user`).

## Job history

runnerd records every job its runners may run in a SQLite database; [`[history]`](../configuration/#history) sets its path and how long each part is kept. For each job:

| What | Kept |
|---|---|
| its timeline: queued, container started, job started, finished, container and runner registration removed | with the job |
| its steps with their start and end, as GitHub shows them | with the job |
| CPU, memory and network of its container, sampled every 2 s | samples for `history.samples`; totals and peaks with the job |
| the bytes the Docker Hub mirror served it | with the job |
| its log, as GitHub shows it with masked secrets still masked, up to `history.log_max` | for `history.logs` |

A job that leaves GitHub's queue without one of this machine's runners is recorded as `elsewhere` (the dashboard's "Not run here"): `cancelled in queue` when its run was cancelled first, as a newer push does, or its conclusion otherwise, such as `skipped`. It has no start, wait or run time, and no median or cost counts it. Only a job another machine's runner took keeps the word `elsewhere`.

A finished job's record goes after `history.jobs`, with its steps and runner. The database file does not shrink when rows go; SQLite reuses the space. runnerd also keeps the sizes of the cache volumes and the slice's CPU and memory over time.

Where the data comes from:

- **Which job a runner took**: a small script runnerd runs in the container (`docker exec`, as root) reads the job's ID from the runner's `_diag/Worker_*.log` within a second of the job starting. GitHub's list of queued jobs still shows the job for several seconds after that, and runnerd does not start a second runner for it.
- **Steps**: GitHub's jobs API, read every poll while a job runs. The runner's own logs name steps differently from GitHub (`Set up runner` for `Set up job`) and change between runner versions. The API's times have second precision.
- **The log**: the runner's `_diag/pages`, which the same script follows. The runner writes the log there in 4 KiB blocks, so lines arrive a block at a time, and a quiet step's lines arrive when it ends.
- **CPU and memory**: the container's and the slice's cgroup files, every 2 s; [Admission and sizing](../admission/#what-a-jobs-memory-is) says what the memory counts. Network from `/proc/<pid>/net/dev`.

## API

JSON on `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/runnerd.sock`, mode 0600: only the user runnerd runs as may connect. The types are in [`crates/core/src/api.rs`](https://github.com/ayagmar/runnerd/blob/main/crates/core/src/api.rs) and its modules. Times are seconds since the Unix epoch, sizes bytes, CPU millicores.

| Request | Response |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/status` | profiles and their slots, running runners, repositories with their pools and queued jobs, each job's pool and why it waits, the memory budget with the mirror's and each seed container's share, what [the host's memory](../admission/#the-hosts-memory) leaves the slice, the slice's CPUs, and under `cpu_overcommit` the CPU budget |
| `POST /v1/repositories/{owner}/{name}/pause?force=` | no new runners; `409` naming the running job, unless `force=true` cancels its run |
| `POST /v1/repositories/{owner}/{name}/resume` | `204` |
| `POST /v1/repositories/pause?force=`, `POST /v1/repositories/resume` | the same for every configured repository; while any runs a job, `409` naming the jobs, and none is paused |
| `POST /v1/profiles/{name}/reseed` | `202`; the profile starts no runner until its slots are free and slot 1 became its snapshot; `409` while it reseeds or a shell is open in one of its records |
| `POST /v1/caches/{name}/clean` | `204`; removes a slot's volume or a shared cache, stopping the runners that wait for a job and use it, warm ones too; `409` naming the jobs that use it |
| `GET /v1/config` | the config file's path, text and revision; notification targets' `url` and header values read `"<secret>"` |
| `POST /v1/config/check` with TOML | what `runnerd check-config` says about it, each `"<secret>"` read as the value it stands for |
| `PUT /v1/config?revision=` with TOML | checks it, with each `"<secret>"` read as the value it stands for, writes it in place of the file and runs with it; invalid (`422`), not writable (`500`) or runnerd stopping (`503`) leaves the file and what runs as they were. With `revision`, from `GET /v1/config`, a config replaced since, as by [auto sizing](../admission/#auto-sizing), or a file edited on disk since, is not overwritten (`409`); the reply's `revision` is that of the text written, to send with the next change |
| `GET /v1/doctor` | `runnerd doctor`'s checks, for the config runnerd runs with |
| `POST /v1/shutdown` with `{"force": false}` | `202` |
| `GET /v1/dashboard` | the dashboard's `[web] open`, as runnerd read it at start |
| `POST /v1/dashboard/sign-out` | `204`; signs every browser out of the dashboard and ends every login link and pairing code made before |
| `POST /v1/dashboard/pairings?origin=` | a 5-minute one-time login link for `origin`, one of `[web] origins`, or one for each of them; `409` when the dashboard is off or has no `[web] origins` |
| `GET /v1/jobs?repository=&state=&conclusion=&workflow=&name=&since=&until=&before=&limit=` | a page of jobs, newest first; `next` is the `before` of the next page |
| `GET /v1/jobs/{id}` | a job with its timeline, steps, metrics and log size |
| `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/samples` | its CPU, memory and network over time |
| `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/logs?from=&follow=` | its log as text; with `follow=true`, open until the job's record is final |
| `GET /v1/host/samples?since=` | the slice's CPU and memory, the last 24 hours at most |
| `GET /v1/stats?repository=&since=&until=&bucket=day` | finished jobs per repository and day (or `hour`), 744 buckets back at most: counts, queue and run time, CPU, memory, mirror bytes |
| `GET /v1/activity?since=` | every job runnerd's runners started or finished from `since` (default 24 hours ago) until now, by when it started or ended rather than when it was queued: each start with how long the job waited, how many finished and failed, and how many of the failed their runner failed: lost with it, or killed for memory at its cap |
| `GET /v1/sizing?since=` | per configured profile and per job (repository, workflow, name): percentiles of the sampled memory and CPU peaks, waits, how busy the slots were, and the [advice](../admission/#sizing) for `memory` and `slots`; whether [auto sizing](../admission/#auto-sizing) is on, the changes it made, and while on what its next look would change, which profiles a recent change holds, which `cpus` raises it cannot make and why, and why no look writes anything when the file on disk was edited since runnerd read it; the last 30 days by default |
| `GET /v1/insights?days=` | what the history says ([Insights](../insights/)): slower, flaky and near-cap jobs, repositories whose downloads memory pressure throttled, waits that got longer, and each repository's waits this week against the week before; 14 days by default, 90 at most |
| `GET /v1/digest` | the last 24 hours: jobs finished and failed, the insights new since the day before, the three longest waits, and the month's [cost estimate](../insights/#what-the-minutes-would-have-cost) |
| `GET /v1/savings` | what this machine's job minutes would have cost on GitHub-hosted runners, this month (UTC) and over the last 30 days, with the jobs left out and why |
| `POST /v1/notify/test?target=` | sends a test message to every `[notify]` target, or the one named, and says what each did; `404` without targets |

| `POST /v1/records/{job}/shell?step=&cols=&rows=` with `Connection: Upgrade`, `Upgrade: runnerd-tty` | `101`, then the connection is the shell's terminal, raw bytes both ways; `409` when the budget is full |

```sh
curl -N --unix-socket "$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/runnerd.sock" http://runnerd/v1/events
```

### Events

| Event | Sent when |
|---|---|
| `job.queued`, `job.started`, `job.step`, `job.finished` | a job moves on |
| `slot.changed` | a runner comes or goes in a slot |
| `profile.changed` | a reload adds or changes a profile, or it starts or stops reseeding |
| `profile.removed` | a reload removed it and its last runner is gone |
| `repository.changed` | paused or resumed, its queue changed, or a reload added or changed it |
| `repository.removed` | a reload removed it |
| `budget.changed`, `cache.reseeded` | the budget changed; a profile has a new snapshot |
| `record.created`, `record.deleted` | a failed job's runner was kept as a record; a record went, and why |
| `sizing.changed` | [auto sizing](../admission/#auto-sizing) wrote a profile's new `memory` and `slots`; it carries the old ones |
| `runner.failed` | a runner could not be registered or started, and the repository gets none for a while; once until one of its runners takes a job again |
| `job.sample`, `host.sample`, `log.line` | live: a sample, or a log line with `logs=true` |

Every event but the live ones has an ID: send the last one seen as `Last-Event-ID` and the stream resumes after it, across daemon restarts. Live events are not stored; a client that missed some reads the job's samples or log.

`slot.changed`, `profile.changed`, `budget.changed` and `repository.changed` carry the state after a change, not each transition: a pause and a resume that land before the daemon publishes its status send nothing. Applying `profile.*` and `slot.changed` to a status's profiles, and `repository.*` to its repositories, keeps them equal to what `/v1/status` returns.

### Pausing

A forced pause cancels the run of each of the repository's running jobs. GitHub cancels runs, not single jobs, so the run's other jobs are cancelled too. The runner reports the job cancelled, which takes under a minute; one still running 90 s later is removed, and its registration is deleted once GitHub lets go of it.

A paused repository's runners still waiting for a job, warm ones included, stop at once, so none takes a job queued before the pause. A pause lasts until a resume, across restarts: the job history records the paused repositories, and a restarted daemon starts no runner for them. If the history cannot record a pause or resume, the change holds until the daemon restarts, and the request fails with `500` saying so. A reload that removes a paused repository forgets its pause; added back, it starts active.

### Reloading

A new config takes effect without a restart, and running jobs keep running. A removed profile, or slots past a lowered `slots`, stay until their runners finish their jobs, and no runner starts in a profile while it runs as many as its `slots`. A runner waiting for a job in a slot past a lowered `slots` is removed. A runner still waiting for a job, warm ones included, is removed when its repository is removed or moved to another profile, or when the repository's `labels` or its profile's `image`, `memory` or `cpus` changed, by hand or by auto sizing. For a new `image`, that waits until Docker has it, so the old runners serve jobs while it pulls. Runners started afterwards get the new limits, image and labels. `history.database`, `caches.build.directory` and `[web]` change only at the next start.
