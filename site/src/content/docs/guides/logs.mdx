Open the job: from the overview's latest jobs, the Jobs page (`?outcome=failure` lists the failed ones), the link in a [notification](../notifications/), or `runnerctl jobs --failed`. A failed job's page opens on what failed: the step, its exit code, its last lines and, while its [record](../records/) is kept, the `runnerctl shell` command.

## The log

The log is folded by step, the failed step open and scrolled to; nested groups fold too. Each line has a link (`#L120`), so a line can be sent to someone. The search finds text in the lines' messages, not their timestamps or `##[…]` commands. Colours come through as the job printed them. While the job runs, **Follow** keeps the newest lines in view.

From a terminal, the TUI's job view shows the same: `enter` folds or opens a step, `/` searches, `n` and `N` go to the next and previous match. `runnerctl logs <id>` prints the plain log; `-f` follows it to the end.

## Compared with an earlier run

The job's page sets it beside the latest earlier success of the same job on its branch: whole-job figures and each step's time, paired by name. In the TUI, `c` does the same.

## Everything else on its page

The page also shows its wait, split at when runnerd first saw the job queued (*Seen by runnerd*, missing for a job a waiting runner took before runnerd listed it, and for jobs older runnerd versions recorded) and when its runner started, its steps on one time axis, memory and CPU against the container's caps (with what [admission](../../concepts/admission/#measured-admission) reserved when that was less), how often the CPU cap held it back, and its latest 30 runs. [Cancel and re-run](../cancel-rerun/) sit at its top.

A job that failed reads `runner fault`, not `failure`, when its failed step's log shows Sysbox losing the runner's seccomp listener: `function not implemented` on a mount, umount or openat2, or `unsafe procfs detected … invalid argument`. Its code did not fail, and a re-run on a new runner is the answer. It still counts as a failure in the stats and notifications.
